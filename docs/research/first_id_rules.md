## 1. Identity and residency documents; is REAL ID needed?

### Rule (plain words)
- **Every CA ID applicant** needs:
  - an SSN (the number, not the card)
  - one identity document showing true full name and date of birth (originals or certified copies only)
  - a thumbprint and a photo
  - the fee, or a reduced/no-fee voucher
- **Residency proof (standard card):** By statute, proof of legal presence and CA residency is required only for an *original* card (VC 12801.5(a)–(c), mirror). The DMV ID page says residency documents are needed "if you have never had a California DL/ID card."
- **Residency proof (REAL ID):** two residency documents are required.

### (a) REAL ID: identity documents (DMV REAL ID checklist, 12 options)
1. Valid U.S. passport or passport card
2. Original or certified U.S. birth certificate. "Abbreviated" or "Abstract" certificates are not accepted.
3. Valid Permanent Resident Card
4. Foreign passport with valid U.S. visa and approved I-94
5. Foreign passport with approved I-94 (FSM, Marshall Islands or Palau only)
6. Certificate of Naturalization or Certificate of U.S. Citizenship
7. Certified birth certificate from a U.S. territory
8. Valid EAD (I-766)
9. Valid PRC, or an expired PRC with an I-797C or I-797
10. Consular Report of Birth Abroad or Certificate of Birth Abroad
11. Valid foreign passport stamped "Processed for I-551"
12. Documents reflecting TPS eligibility

If your name has changed, also bring a certified marriage certificate, domestic partnership record, court adoption record or divorce record.

### (a) REAL ID: residency documents (two required, 20 options)
1. Utility or cell phone bill
2. Bank or credit card records
3. Insurance documents
4. Medical documents
5. Any U.S. government agency document
6. Mortgage bill
7. Employment documents
8. IRS or FTB tax return
9. Faith-based organization document
10. Signed lease
11. School documents (must show date of birth)
12. USPS change-of-address confirmation
13. Property tax bill
14. **Letter attesting CA residence** from a homeless shelter, abused women's shelter, nonprofit, faith-based organization, employer or government agency
15. Deed or title
16. Voter registration confirmation
17. Proof of CA resident tuition
18. BOE-266
19. Court documents listing the applicant as a CA resident
20. Vehicle or vessel title or registration
21. **A completed and signed DL 933**

Tracing documents can link a residency document in someone else's name to the applicant: a birth certificate, marriage license or DP certificate. For minors, the foster care forms SOC 154, 154A and 156 also work.

P.O. Box: it can be your mailing address, but one residency document must show both the P.O. Box and a physical address.

### (b) Standard "Federal Limits Apply" card
**Identity / legal presence documents (DMV DL 1144, Rev. 11/2025, Section 1):**
1. Certified U.S. birth certificate
2. U.S. passport or passport card
3. CRBA or Certificate of Birth Abroad
4. BIA Proof of Indian Blood Degree
5. Military ID, CAC or DD-214
6. American Indian Card
7. U.S. Citizen ID Card
8. Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship
9. Northern Mariana Card
10. Mexican Border Crossing Card with I-94
11. Canadian birth certificate
12. PRC
13. Temporary Resident ID Card
14. Nonresident Alien Canadian Border Crossing Card
15. Foreign passport with approved I-94
16. EAD (I-766)
17. Foreign passport stamped "Processed for I-551"
18. Re-entry Permit
19. Refugee Travel Document
20. I-94 stamped refugee, parolee, asylee or similar
21. I-94 with photo stamped as temporary evidence of LPR status
22. Canadian passport
23. Immigration judge's order granting asylum
24. **"Certified order/judgment issued from a court of competent jurisdiction, signed by the judge… stating the applicant's name, birth date, and place of birth or legal presence status"**
25. I-797 approved petition
26. **"Release Identification Card issued by the Federal Bureau of Prisons."**

**Residency documents (DL 1144 Section 2):** the same categories as the REAL ID list, including the shelter or nonprofit letter and DL 933. DL 1144 says: "DMV will not accept an application without one accompanying residency document." DMV materials disagree on this point:
- The ID card page says residency documents are needed only if you never had a CA DL/ID.
- DL 1144 says one document is required.
- Fast Facts FFDL 6 says two documents are needed "if applying for an ID."

App guidance: bring at least one; bring two to be safe.

### Is REAL ID needed for this population?
Mostly no. DMV: "No. A REAL ID driver's license or identification card is optional."

You do **not** need a REAL ID to:
- apply for or receive federal benefits (VA, SSA)
- enter a post office or other federal facility that doesn't require ID
- visit a hospital
- ride Amtrak or other public ground transportation

A REAL ID, or an alternative such as a passport, **is** needed to:
- board domestic flights (TSA: enforcement began May 7, 2025)
- enter secure federal facilities "such as military bases, federal courthouses"

A Federal Limits Apply card works as photo ID but "not as evidence of legal presence."

## 2. Birth certificate from LA County: ID and notarization

### Rule
**In person at LA County RR/CC:**
- Authorized certified copy: $34. LA says "Remember to bring a valid photo ID."
- Records from 1962 to present (except 1972–77) are handed over at the appointment.

The statute does not itself require photo ID in person. HSC 103526(a)(2) (mirror) says that for in-person requests the official takes a sworn statement that the applicant is an authorized person. No notarization is needed in person. LA's photo-ID requirement is county practice. Whether LA offers an in-person path for someone with no photo ID is **UNCONFIRMED**.

**By mail:**
- The Certificate of Identity (sworn statement) "must be notarized or it will delay the process."
- Payment by check or money order.
- Mailed within 20 business days.
- Statute: mail, fax and electronic requests need a notarized sworn statement (HSC 103526(a)(1)).

**Online (VitalChek):**
- "The notarized Certificate of Identity form may be requested to complete the order."
- Statute allows remote identity verification meeting NIST 800-63A IAL2 instead (HSC 103526(a)(3)). If identity can't be verified that way, a notarized statement is required.

**Informational copy:** needs no sworn statement, but is stamped "INFORMATIONAL, NOT A VALID DOCUMENT TO ESTABLISH IDENTITY." **Useless for DMV.**

**The notary needs satisfactory evidence of identity** (see item 3).

## 3. Notary identification through credible witnesses (Civ. Code 1185)

### Rule (mirror text, section last updated 2017-01-01)
A notary may identify a signer through credible witnesses only in two ways.

**(b)(1) One credible witness.** The witness must be **personally known to the notary**. The witness must **also prove their own identity with a qualifying ID**. They swear or affirm all of the following:
- the signer is the person named in the document
- the witness personally knows the signer
- in the witness's reasonable belief, the signer's circumstances make it very difficult or impossible to obtain another ID
- the signer has none of the IDs listed in (b)(3) or (b)(4)
- the witness has no financial interest in the document and is not named in it

**(b)(2) Two credible witnesses.** They need not be known to the notary, but **each must present a qualifying ID**. Each swears under penalty of perjury to the same statements.

The draft was close, but it missed three points:
- Witnesses must swear that the signer has no ID and can't readily get one.
- The witness may not have a financial interest in the document or be named in it.
- Even the single "personally known" witness must show ID.

**IDs a notary may accept** must be current or **issued within the last five years**.
- **Under (b)(3):**
  - CA DMV DL or ID card
  - U.S. passport
  - **CDCR inmate ID, if the person is in state prison**
  - **sheriff's inmate ID, if the person is in a local detention facility**
- **Under (b)(4)** (must have photo, description, signature and serial number):
  - foreign passport or consular ID
  - out-of-state, Canadian or Mexican DL
  - out-of-state ID card
  - U.S. military ID
  - CA state or local government employee ID
  - tribal ID

So an **expired CA DL or ID issued within 5 years** still works at a notary. That gives a useful edge in the rules graph.

### Source
- Civ. Code 1185 (mirror: https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_1185). Official: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1185 (could not be fetched)

**Confidence:** medium-high (mirror text; re-verify the verbatim wording on leginfo).

## 4. Replacing a CA DL or ID without a birth certificate

### Rule
**Statute:** VC 12801.5 (mirror) requires proof of legal presence and CA residency for an **original** DL/ID. The section is silent on renewals and duplicates.

**DMV practice:**
- ID cards **cannot be replaced online**: "ID cards and commercial driver's licenses (CDL) are not eligible for this application."
- You submit a new online application, then visit an office.
- At the office you pay the fee, and DMV validates your photograph and scans your thumbprint.
- DMV's replacement page does **not** list identity documents for a replacement. It says nothing about how old the record can be, or whether REAL ID status carries over.

**Evidence that the photo on file matters:**
- The $8 county-jail replacement (VC 14902(g), mirror) requires a "usable photo on file … no more than 10 years old," prior CA DL/ID, no outstanding fees and verified identity.
- CDCR's Cal-ID path (PC 3007.05(c)) requires a usable photo on file, with a new photo taken if it is unusable, and DMV-verified name, DOB, SSN and legal presence.

**UNCONFIRMED:** an explicit general DMV rule that anyone who ever held a CA DL/ID can always replace it without a birth certificate. The rule may depend on the age of the photo, or on whether legal presence was verified on the old record. App guidance: "likely no birth certificate needed if DMV has your photo and thumbprint on file; bring any ID document you have."

### Sources
- https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-licenses-identification-cards/replace-your-driver-license-or-identification-dl-id-card (modified 2026-08-07). Quote: "ID cards and commercial driver's licenses (CDL) are not eligible for this application."
- VC 12801.5 and VC 14902(g) (mirrors above)

**Confidence:** medium for the online and office steps (high). **Low** for the general "no birth certificate needed" claim.

**Confirmed.**

## 6. DL 933 (no fee), DL 937 (reduced fee), DL 932, senior ID

### Correction: the draft has the form numbers wrong
- **DL 933** = "No Fee Identification Card Eligibility Verification" (homeless)
- **DL 937** = "Verification for Reduced Fee Identification Card" (public assistance)
- **DL 932** = the *organization's* "Order Request" form, used to register an organization and order DL 933 and DL 937 forms. **It is not the reduced-fee form.**

### No fee (DL 933)
**Who qualifies:** anyone who is "homeless" under McKinney-Vento (42 U.S.C. 11301 et seq.). DMV's own page says this includes "a homeless child or youth … or an individual or family fleeing domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking, or another dangerous, life-threatening condition."
- No fee for an original **or replacement** ID.
- The verifier may not charge for verification (VC 14902(d), mirror).

**Who may verify (statute, VC 14902(f)(3), mirror):**
- a government or nonprofit agency funded to serve homeless people, or sanctioned by the local Continuum of Care
- a CA-licensed attorney
- an LEA homeless liaison or school social worker
- a state-funded human or social services provider serving homeless youth
- a police or sheriff officer designated as homeless liaison
- others DMV qualifies

**DMV practice is narrower:**
- "Only Government Public Social Service Agencies and IRS 501(c)(3) tax-exempt nonprofit organizations that serve low-income individuals and/or the unhoused can participate."
- The organization must register through DL 932.
- Nonprofits must show government homeless funding or CoC membership.
- DL 932 also names educational liaisons, attorneys and law enforcement as possible verifiers.
- Forms "must not be shared, photocopied, or uploaded online."
- Forms must be "submitted to DMV not more than 90 days after being issued."
- Individuals cannot get forms directly. They must go through a registered organization.

**A DL 933 also counts as a residency document** (see items 1 and 16).

### Reduced fee (DL 937)
- Covers people whose income qualifies them for county or state assistance. The statute refers to the programs by Welfare & Institutions Code chapter.
- DMV's organization page lists CalWORKs, CalFresh, county General Assistance, CFAP and CAPI.
- A government or nonprofit entity completes the DL 937.
- Current DMV fee: $11.

### Senior ID
- Free for applicants 62 or older.
- "valid for eight birthdates" (DMV page summary).

### Sources
- https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-licenses-identification-cards/reduced-no-fee-id-card-program-information-for-organizations/ (modified 2025-07-29)
- https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/uploads/2023/09/DL-932-R8-2023-AS-WWW.pdf (DL 932, Rev. 8/2023)
- https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-licenses-identification-cards/identification-id-cards/ (2026-08-07). Quotes: "Verification for Reduced Fee Identification Card (DL 937)" and "To qualify for a no-fee senior citizen ID card, you must be at least 62 years old."
- VC 14902 (mirrors: https://california.public.law/codes/vehicle_code_section_14902 and https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/vehicle-code/veh-sect-14902/, "current as of January 01, 2026")

**Confidence:** high.

## 7. AB 1733: free birth certificate for homeless people (HSC 103577)

### Corrections
- **AB 1733 (Quirk-Silva) is Chapter 764, Statutes of 2014**, approved 2014-09-29. It is not a 2015 law.
- The free birth record started **on or after July 1, 2015**.
- The free ID card started **on and after January 1, 2016**.

### Rule (HSC 103577, mirror, updated 2019-01-01)
**Who must issue it free:** the local registrar, county recorder **or State Registrar** must give a certified birth record free to someone verified as a homeless person or homeless child or youth (McKinney-Vento definition).
- One record per application.
- No verification fee may be charged.
- The State Registrar must provide up to 3 free certified copies per year, more at its discretion (103577(d)).

**How status is verified:** by an affidavit that CDPH develops. It "will not be considered complete unless signed by both" the requester and a homeless services provider. The list of qualifying providers is the same as in VC 14902(f).

**Form:** CDPH "AFFIDAVIT OF HOMELESS STATUS FOR FEE EXEMPT CERTIFIED COPY OF BIRTH CERTIFICATE" (Rev. 10/18). No form number was found. Both signers sign under "swear or affirm, to the best of my knowledge and belief." The form does not mention notarization.

**Minors:** the youth may request their own record. There is no age limit, and an unaccompanied minor needs no parent signature (SchoolHouse Connection summary; secondary source).

**The law waives the fee, not the identity rules.** HSC 103577 does not exempt the request from HSC 103526. An authorized copy still needs the sworn statement: in person in front of staff, or notarized by mail. One county application form says a free copy requires "additional forms." Whether LA County RR/CC waives its photo-ID rule for these requests is **UNCONFIRMED**.

**Where to apply:** CDPH says requests may go to the county of birth or the State Registrar ("Customers may now apply at the state level as well," citing AB 2490).

### Sources
- https://www.legiscan.com/CA/text/AB1733/2013 (Ch. 764, Stats. 2014). Quote: "would, on or after July 1, 2015, require each local registrar or county recorder to issue, without a fee, a certified record of live birth"
- https://california.public.law/codes/ca_health_and_safety_code_section_103577
- https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CHSI/Pages/Assembly-Bill-(AB)-1733.aspx
- CDPH affidavit, as posted by El Dorado County: https://www.eldoradocounty.ca.gov/files/assets/county/v/1/documents/records-request/affidavit-for-free-certified-copy-of-birth-certificate-8-15.pdf (Rev. 10/18)

**Confidence:** high for the statute and form. Medium on the notarization point, which is inferred from 103526.

## 9. Free IDs for everyone or for youth; AB 1766

### Rule
**AB 1766 (Stone, 2022)**, chaptered 2022-09-23 (chapter number **UNCONFIRMED**), does **not** make ID cards free. It requires DMV to issue an ID card to people who cannot prove legal presence, if they prove identity and CA residency, **"no later than July 1, 2027"** (VC 12801.9(a)(2), mirror, updated 2024-01-01). An earlier draft said January 1, 2024; the Aug 11, 2022 amendment moved the deadline to July 1, 2027.
- The card must state it "is not acceptable for official federal purposes."
- **Acceptable documents (VC 12801.9(c)):**
  - consular ID or foreign passport
  - birth certificate
  - foreign DL
  - foreign electoral card
  - school records
  - I-589
  - and others

DMV's actual launch date and whether it is on schedule are **UNCONFIRMED**. No DMV announcement was found.

**Free ID for all or for youth:** **No such law found.** As of 2026-08-07, DMV's fee page lists only:
- regular ID: $40
- reduced fee: $11
- senior: free
- homeless: free

AB 962 (Fong, 2023–24) would have cut fees by $2 starting Jan 1, 2027. It was **vetoed**. **UNCONFIRMED** that no 2025–26 bill created a free youth ID, because the search budget ran out.

## 10. Foster youth

### Rule
**WIC 391** (mirror, updated 2022-01-01). The county welfare department must verify that the youth received certain documents. The required list grows with age:
- **At the first review hearing after age 16:**
  - SSN card
  - copy of the birth certificate
  - DL or ID card
- **At the last review hearing before age 18:**
  - SSN card
  - **certified** birth certificate
  - DL or ID card
  - Medi-Cal BIC
  - proof of citizenship or legal residence, if applicable
  - a county letter confirming foster care dates
  - others
- **Before jurisdiction ends for a nonminor dependent:** all of the above must be verified again.

The statute requires the county to make sure the youth *has* these documents. It **does not itself waive DMV fees.**

**Free birth certificate for foster youth: HSC 103578** (AB 2967, Ch. 551, Stats. 2018, operative July 1, 2019).
- Local registrars and county recorders must issue a certified birth record free to "a youth who has been placed in foster care" (WIC 11400(f)).
- Verification is a CDPH affidavit signed by the requester **and the county welfare agency**.
- The statute names local registrars and county recorders, **not** the State Registrar.
- Whether *former* foster youth qualify is not stated (**UNCONFIRMED**).

**ID card fee:**
- There is **no foster-youth-specific free ID** in VC 14902.
- Foster youth can qualify for **reduced fee (DL 937)** as recipients of AFDC-FC or Kin-GAP. CDSS ACIN I-67-05 (2005) lists them, and VC 14902(c) covers W&I Chapter 2, which includes foster care.
- They get a **no-fee ID** if they meet the McKinney-Vento homeless definition (DL 933).

- https://california.public.law/codes/ca_health_and_safety_code_section_103578

## 11. Reentry: CDCR Cal-ID (PC 3007.05) and LA County jail

### Rule
**PC 3007.05** (mirror, updated 2023-01-01):
- CDCR and DMV "shall ensure" that an eligible inmate released from state prison has a valid CA ID.
- CDCR must help get the birth certificate and SSN records, including notary services and forms, "to the extent administratively feasible."
- **Eligibility, (c) prior CA DL/ID:**
  - usable photo on file (a new photo is taken if not)
  - DMV-verified name, DOB and SSN
  - legal presence verified, or identity under VC 12801.9 once that is implemented
  - CA residency
- **Eligibility, (d) never held a CA DL/ID:**
  - application signed under penalty of perjury
  - photo
  - thumbprint
  - proof of the same information
- **Fee:** $8 (VC 14902(h)).
- **Fallback (g):** if no ID is obtained before release, CDCR issues a photo prison ID card.

**CDCR's own page** (modified 2026-08-27) is narrower than the statute:
- Lists "Have a Social Security Number (SSN)" and previous issuance of a CA ID or DL.
- "Available within 13 months of release."

Discrepancy: the statute's (d) path for people who never held an ID does not appear on CDCR's page.

**Federal prison:** a "Release Identification Card issued by the Federal Bureau of Prisons" is an acceptable identity document for a standard card (DL 1144).

**Notary inside prison or jail:** a CDCR or sheriff inmate ID counts as notary ID while the person is in custody (Civ. Code 1185(b)(3)).

**County jail, including LA:**

### Rule

## 14. Replacing a Social Security card; SSA identity with no ID

### Rule
**Replacement card basics:**
- A replacement card is **free**.
- Some people can apply online; otherwise you need an appointment at a local office. "Your local Social Security office cannot print one for you."
- The card arrives by mail in 5–10 business days.
- Limits: 3 per year and 10 per lifetime (20 CFR 422.103(e)(2)).
  - Name changes and certain immigration-status changes don't count toward the limits.
  - "Significant hardship" exceptions exist, for example a referral letter from a governmental social services agency.

**Identity evidence (20 CFR 422.107(c)):** "convincing evidence of your identity" showing your name plus either age/DOB/parents' names or a photo/description. **"A birth record is not sufficient evidence to establish identity."**

**SSA's priority list** (POMS RM 10210.420, effective 07/31/2026):
- **Primary (unexpired only):**
  - U.S. DL
  - state non-driver ID
  - U.S. passport
- **Secondary** (allowed only when primary evidence can't be obtained within 10 business days):
  - military ID
  - naturalization or citizenship certificate
  - tribal card
  - government or private employee ID
  - **certified medical record** showing name and DOB or age
  - **health insurance or Medicaid card** with photo or DOB
  - current-year school ID, certified school record or transcript
  - and others
- **No-ID path:** "third-level evidence" handled case by case. The details are **UNCONFIRMED**.
- Photocopies are not accepted unless certified by the custodian of record.

**Practical graph edges:**
- Medi-Cal BIC with DOB, or a certified clinic or hospital record, gets you to an SSN card.
- A school record or transcript works for teens.

## 15. Safe at Home; substitute address on a DMV ID

### Rule
**Safe at Home** (SOS address confidentiality program):
- **Who can enroll:**
  - survivors of DV, sexual assault, stalking, human trafficking, child abduction, or elder or dependent adult abuse
  - reproductive and gender-affirming care providers, workers and patients
  - certain public employees
- **How:** enrollment is in person through a community-based Enrolling Agency (Gov. Code 6206). Certification lasts **4 years**.
- **Agencies:** state and local agencies must accept the SOS substitute address when creating or keeping public records, with narrow exceptions (Gov. Code 6207(a)–(b)).

**DMV is special-cased.** Gov. Code 6207(e) (mirror) says participants who want DMV records suppressed must follow VC 1808.21(a), and must still meet other Vehicle Code requirements for giving DMV their current address.
- VC 1808.21: DMV residence addresses are already confidential.
- A Safe at Home participant card gets the whole record suppressed for 4-year renewable periods.
- Without enrollment, police or court documents of stalking or threats get 1-year suppression.

Whether DMV will **print the Safe at Home substitute address** on the card, or accept it as the residence address, is **UNCONFIRMED**. The statute suggests DMV still collects the real residence address and keeps it confidential. The address printed on the card is the mailing address. A P.O. Box is allowed if one residency document shows both the P.O. Box and a physical address (DL 1010 E).

**Fee:** DV survivors who are fleeing qualify for the **no-fee** ID as "homeless" under McKinney-Vento, per DMV's own wording.

### Sources
- https://www.sos.ca.gov/registries/safe-home. Quote: address is "accepted by California state, county, and city government agencies"
- https://california.public.law/codes/ca_gov't_code_section_6206 and https://california.public.law/codes/ca_gov't_code_section_6207
- https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_1808.21
- DMV ID card page (item 1)

**Confidence:** medium. Whether DMV accepts the substitute address on the card is UNCONFIRMED.

## 16. Proof of residency for unhoused people

### Rule
Accepted for both REAL ID and standard cards:
- **a letter on letterhead from a homeless shelter, abused women's shelter, nonprofit, faith-based organization, employer or government agency** attesting that the applicant lives in California
- **a completed and signed DL 933**

Other low-barrier options:
- medical documents
- a typed government agency document (for example a benefits letter)
- a CA court document listing the applicant as a resident
- for minors: tracing to a parent's document, or foster forms SOC 154, 154A or 156

Count:
- REAL ID needs **two** different residency documents. A shelter letter plus a DL 933 appear to be two separate list items, but **UNCONFIRMED** whether DMV accepts that pair.
- Standard card: see item 1. Residency documents are needed only for an original card per the DMV page; DL 1144 requires one.
